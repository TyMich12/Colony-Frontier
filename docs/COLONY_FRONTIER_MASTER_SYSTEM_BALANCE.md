# Colony-Frontier — Master System Performance & Upgrade Table

**Status:** shared ratings, retrofit progress, and repair choices are connected to live inspection and simulation. The secondary numerical effects below remain design targets unless a corresponding live simulation effect is explicitly implemented. Research-tier values supersede the earlier Research column. These are game-balance targets under standard reference conditions, not claims that one real spacecraft design would achieve every rating.

## Current mission power tuning

- Reference life-support demand is **1.30 kW** for shared habitat loads plus **1.45 kW** for air revitalisation. The per-system base, tier, and retrofit draw estimates were increased by 20% to make equipment operation a more meaningful trade-off.
- Mission 2's Blackout scenario starts with one simulated Earth day of sunlight, with the Sun moving from sunrise to sunset. The following 13 days are intentionally dark; the power panel labels this state, and optional loads shed as the battery reaches 95% charge.
- At the 2.75 kW life-support load, 13 dark days require about **903 kWh** from the battery after 95% discharge efficiency. A full 1,200 kWh Foundation battery therefore ends near **24.7% charge**, just above the protected 20% reserve. This is a no-incident planning estimate; repairs and load shedding remain important.
- Missions after Mission 1 apply a slightly stronger stage-pressure increase: 6.5% per stage, capped at 80%, with the existing Mars pressure adjustment unchanged.

## How to read the table

- Every triplet is **0/2 → 1/2 → 2/2** for that tier.
- Tier order is **Foundation → Frontier → Apex → Research**.
- A tier is a major equipment replacement / technology-generation jump. Its 0/2 value should normally exceed the previous tier's 2/2 value by a clear margin.
- A retrofit is a small, locally installed improvement. Its steps should be smaller than a tier jump and have a concrete mechanism.
- “Higher is better” metrics increase; “lower is better” metrics decrease.
- Daily throughput uses a standard 24-hour simulation day. It must be converted to the game's accelerated clock consistently.
- These are target values. Do not combine them with old direct-gain retrofit bonuses until the implementation is reconciled; otherwise improvements may be counted twice.

## What each tier means

| Tier | Engineering meaning | What should change in gameplay |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Robust, relatively simple, Earth-manufactured baseline hardware | Affordable and maintainable, but lower throughput, efficiency, redundancy, or precision. Suitable for getting the outpost running, not comfortably supporting every optional system at once. |
| **Frontier** | Improved components, better controls and materials, more capable actuators/processors | A meaningful step up: greater throughput, lower losses, better reliability, and fewer bottlenecks. It should be clearly better than a fully retrofitted Foundation system. |
| **Apex** | High-performance hardware with advanced control, improved thermal/material design, and stronger fault tolerance | Large gains to capacity and efficiency; supports heavier workloads and more demanding mission tasks. Higher purchase cost, complexity, and potentially higher electrical draw are reasonable trade-offs. |
| **Research** | Cutting-edge, tightly integrated experimental equipment | The largest capability jump and best specialist performance. May be expensive, harder to maintain, use more power, or require advanced support systems. It should feel exceptional, not magically free of constraints. |

## What each retrofit level means

- **0/2 — stock hardware:** no retrofit installed yet.
- **1/2 — first field retrofit:** one focused improvement, such as better seals, a calibrated controller, a filter insert, a sensor, or a replaceable wear component.
- **2/2 — second field retrofit:** a further compatible improvement to the same system, not a duplicate full upgrade. It should provide a smaller additional gain than changing to a higher tier.

A retrofit is a discrete installed improvement. It uses commissioning energy and simulation time. It must **not** silently repair damage, apply an unrelated percentage multiplier, or give the same benefit twice.

## Balancing rules for remaining implementation

1. **One authoritative number per metric and state.** The live simulation, system inspector, retrofit preview, and status dashboard should all read the same value.
2. **Do not stack old direct gains on top of this table.** Decide whether a retrofit changes the selected table state or adds a separately displayed effect. Do one, not both.
3. **Tier changes must be a real equipment choice.** They should affect the appropriate capabilities and can carry mass, credit, support, and electrical-demand trade-offs.
4. **Capacity is not consumption.** Solar output, battery capacity, battery charge/discharge power, grid transfer capacity, and system electrical draw are separate quantities.
5. **Respect physical caps.** Percentages cannot exceed 100%; purity/efficiency values near 100% should have diminishing returns. A shield's event-specific protection is not universal radiation protection.
6. **Use the right clock and units.** Do not label a 24-hour throughput as per 10-second simulation tick. Convert it explicitly at the simulation boundary.
7. **Lower-is-better metrics must stay lower after upgrades.** Examples: habitat leak rate, navigation error, and water use per kg food.
8. **Power budget must be rebalanced after tier effects are connected.** Higher-tier equipment may draw more electricity; an upgrade must not increase output while accidentally leaving all operating costs unchanged.
9. **The standalone repair/mitigation option remains genuinely standalone.** It must require no other systems; optional/integrated options may use their stated support systems.
10. **These are balance targets, not universal scientific specifications.** In particular, research-tier range, throughput, purity, and accuracy need mission-specific plausibility checks.

### Tier upgrades also need explicit numerical effects

A tier upgrade is a **new equipment package**, not a free retrofit. When a system is changed to the next tier, replace the old tier's rating with the new tier's **0/2** rating, then apply only the retrofits installed for that new tier. Do not carry the old tier's retrofit bonuses forward.

In addition to the rating changes in the master table, the following are proposed tier-level trade-offs to make upgrades tangible:

| Tier change | Equipment / gameplay change |
|---|---|
| Foundation → Frontier | Installed unit mass **+15%**; purchase cost **+35%**; power draw for powered equipment **+5%** unless the higher tier's efficiency metric explicitly offsets it |
| Frontier → Apex | Installed unit mass **+20%**; purchase cost **+50%**; powered-equipment draw **+10%** unless efficiency offsets it |
| Apex → Research | Installed unit mass **+25%**; purchase cost **+75%**; powered-equipment draw **+15%** unless efficiency offsets it |

These are **provisional balance rules**, not yet code. Applying one generic draw multiplier to every system would be too crude: solar arrays, habitats, passive shielding, batteries, and powered machinery do not share one electrical-load behaviour. Before implementation, define tier-specific operating draw in **kW for each powered system** and leave passive systems' draw unchanged unless they have powered subsystems.

### Worked example: Solar Power

- **Foundation 0/2:** rated output **12.0 kW**, solar-dust index **D** as measured by the environment.
- **Foundation 1/2:** rated output **12.4 kW**; retrofit also reduces dust index by **15 points**.
- **Foundation 2/2:** rated output **12.8 kW**; dust index is reduced by **25 points total** from the 0/2 state; pointing calibration improves error by **2°**.
- **Frontier 0/2:** the tier change replaces the Foundation array with a **20.0 kW** rated array; Foundation retrofit gains do not carry over.
- Live solar generation is calculated from rated output and the existing illumination, temperature, dust, pointing, and condition factors. The **+0.4 kW rating change and dust reduction are distinct effects**, but the dust reduction must be applied only once.

### Implementation gate

Before these secondary numerical effects are wired into gameplay, check each one against the existing simulation state and UI. Where the game has no variable yet (for example jam rate, sensor drift, or diagnostic sensitivity), add an explicit variable and visible feedback or omit that secondary effect from the first implementation pass. Do not display an effect as working until the simulation actually applies it.

- The four effect values are **Foundation / Frontier / Apex / Research** values for that repair's named metric, in the listed unit. They are direct numerical values, not an invented generic performance percentage.

- Required systems are support systems that must be available for the procedure. Standalone has no required support system. Enhanced has one; Dual has two; Integrated has three.
- Secondary and tertiary effects are separate metrics and units, not extra gains to the primary metric.
- Respect the option's cap and remaining fault. Percentages cannot exceed 100%; outputs cannot exceed rated system capacity; a repair cannot restore more than the fault requires.
- Repair and retrofit procedures are activated using energy and simulated time; optional support systems are selected as part of the procedure.

## Electrical demand model — mission-average reference (updated 2026-10-15)

**Preservation rule:** This section supplements the existing system ratings, retrofit, repair, costs, durations and mission tables. It does not change any system output rating or any Apex/Research tier values. The demand model is an explicit mission-average engineering/game estimate; it is not a certified hardware specification.

### Why the demand calculation changed

The previous demand model treated several cyclic systems as if they drew their reference power continuously and used a 2.55 kW shared core load whose label also implied life-support/electrolysis. That made the starting colony's average demand too high compared with a 12 kW peak-rated Foundation solar array. The revised model separates shared habitat controls from dedicated air revitalisation, water treatment and thermal control, and uses explicit **average kW values** for systems that operate intermittently. It does not use hidden multipliers or make demand fluctuate with sunlight, battery charge, repair condition, animation frames or simulation ticks.

These values are suitable for the game's reference-colony balance, not universal spacecraft hardware facts. Actual electrical demand depends on hardware architecture, duty cycle, crew size, thermal environment and operating schedule. Peak loads must be handled by local electronics/storage and may exceed the mission-average values shown here.

### Reference base loads and tier/retrofit additions

At Foundation 0/2, use the base load below. Each technology tier step adds the listed absolute kW value, and each installed retrofit level adds the listed absolute kW value. Foundation → Frontier → Apex → Research is three tier steps; retrofit level is 0, 1 or 2. Do not use percentages or hidden performance multipliers.

### Deterministic calculation and validation rules

1. For each installed optional system: **rated load = base load + (tier index × tier-step kW) + (retrofit level × retrofit-step kW)**. Foundation = 0, Frontier = 1, Apex = 2, Research = 3. Clamp retrofit level to 0–2.
2. Count combined air revitalisation **once**, if either oxygen or CO₂ control is installed. Use the higher installed tier and the higher retrofit level for its load.
3. Displayed demand is the sum of shared core load plus all installed-system rated loads. The displayed breakdown must sum to the same total, subject only to UI rounding.
4. Solar illumination, dust, temperature, pointing, faults and array condition affect generation—not demand. Battery state, efficiency, limits and available energy affect battery flow—not demand.
5. Battery charging is a destination for surplus power and battery discharge is a separate source. Neither is added to rated system demand.
6. Emergency load shedding changes **served load**, not the rated requested-demand display. Show any curtailed loads separately.
7. Demand changes only when systems are installed/removed, a system's tier changes, or a retrofit is commissioned. Do not recalculate it from live generation, SOC or animation/tick timing.
8. Keep two decimal places for kW consistently. Avoid random values and hidden multipliers.
9. The Foundation agriculture *rated table value* remains **0.50 kg/day** and is preserved for tier comparisons. Mission 1 adds 0.55 kW of grow lighting; the simulation scales yield in proportion to the Foundation baseline specific energy (0.75 kW for 0.50 kg/day), giving about **0.87 kg/day at 100% condition after crop maturity**. Crop output still ramps with maturity, falls with equipment condition, and stops when the farm is unpowered. This is a small scenario-specific lighting addition, not a change to the underlying tier table.
10. These are mission-average game assumptions pending a specified hardware design. They should not be presented as experimentally verified flight-hardware specifications.

### Solar and sustainability interpretation

The Foundation solar array remains **12 kW rated peak output**. Live output continues to be calculated from the array rating and illumination, temperature, dust, pointing, condition and power-conversion effects. A 12 kW nameplate is not 12 kW continuously available, and no fixed output should be forced into the display.

With the revised base loads, the reference colony's core + air revitalisation + water + thermal + compulsory agriculture is about **4.95 kW** before communications, navigation, controllers or optional equipment. This is a more credible starting point for a daylight power budget, while optional science, rover work, extraction and ISRU can still create a deficit and be shed or scheduled. It does **not** claim that a 1,200 kWh Foundation battery can sustain every system through the full 14-Earth-day lunar night; nighttime survival still requires carefully managed loads and sufficient storage or another dependable power source.

This demand-model update changes **no resource-output target, repair effect, retrofit effect, costs/durations, or existing tier output ratings**, including Apex and Research values. Foundation grid capacity and solar reference settings remain as previously specified.

## Level 1 first-run balance, resource simulation and live telemetry — updated 2026-10-16

This section documents the current MissionSimulation implementation. It supersedes the older Mission 1 tutorial water/food safeguards described in earlier revisions. It does **not** change the tier-rating tables above; in particular, all Apex and Research values remain untouched.

### Shared condition and operating-state rules

- Live output/capacity is condition-adjusted where applicable: **effective output = rated output × (condition / 100)**. System-specific factors (such as sunlight, dust, faults, temperature, pointing, power availability and throughput limits) are applied separately.
- A system that is offline or does not receive sufficient power does not receive free production or recovery.
- Inspectors should display the same live values used by the simulation, including current status, relevant physical readings, reserves/throughput and fault state.
- Repair/retrofit workflows use their selected activation energy, simulation duration and support-system requirements. The standalone option remains genuinely standalone. Mission 1 must not require spare parts or other extra equipment/materials in its loadout or action interface.

### Solar generation

- Solar generation is calculated from the selected solar installation's rated output and the live operating conditions: sunlight availability, equipment condition, cell-temperature loss, dust transmission, array pointing error and solar-specific fault/degradation penalties.
- An unrelated system's general operational/dependency flag must not force solar output to zero. Solar should generate whenever the array is selected and healthy enough, daylight is available, and no active solar-specific fault prevents generation.
- A displayed value of **0.00 kW is valid** during lunar night/no illumination, when the solar installation is not selected, or when an active solar fault prevents generation. Condition, dust, pointing and temperature should degrade output continuously; do not use an arbitrary 25% condition cutoff.
- Solar dust accumulation and cleaning remain part of the live output calculation. Solar diagnostics may show estimated damaged strings/hot spots and pointing/calibration error; these are model estimates, not literal sensor-grade hardware measurements.

### Water recovery — recovered water, not generated water

- The Water system does **not** create new water. It returns a percentage of water that was used and processed back into the reserve.
- The crew recoverable/make-up stream is **8 L/day for four crew**. Crop water use is added using actual food production multiplied by the selected Agriculture system's water-use rating.
- **Water recovered = min(total water used, available treatment throughput) × effective recovery percentage.** Treatment throughput is a processing-capacity limit, not a water source.
- **Net reserve change = water recovered − total water used.** The reserve falls by the unrecovered amount; no tutorial local-volatile feed or free 6 L/day water source is applied.
- Example: if 10 L is used during the day and the effective recovery rate is 60%, with enough powered treatment throughput, 6 L is recovered and 4 L is lost from the reserve.
- Effective recovery is the selected system's rated recovery efficiency adjusted for condition and capped at 100%. It receives **no Mission 1 +45 percentage-point bonus**. If the Water system is offline or underpowered, effective recovery is zero.
- Live telemetry should distinguish **water used today**, **water recovered today**, **effective recovery (%)**, **treatment throughput**, **crop water use**, **crew water stream**, and **net water balance**.

### Food production and crew demand

- Four-crew food demand is **2.48 kg/day** (0.62 kg per crew member per day, expressed as edible dry-food equivalent).
- Food production is based on the selected Agriculture system's rated yield, equipment condition and available power. The former Mission 1 artificial production floor and extra tutorial production boost are removed.
- Crop production ramps up as crops mature rather than instantly producing the full rated yield: `cropMaturity = clamp((elapsedDays − 0.5) / 2.5, 0, 1)`. Production is multiplied by this maturity fraction, so the system reaches its rated condition-adjusted output after the ramp.
- Food reserve capacity is **120 kg**. The initial reserve is set by the mission's starting-reserve percentage. Food reserves are consumed at 2.48 kg/day and replenished only by actual crop production.
- Agriculture's water use is based on actual crop output, so a lower crop yield also reduces crop water demand. Do not reintroduce a tutorial minimum yield that masks a food deficit.

- Continue to use the current Mission 1 incident pacing, reduced condition wear and smaller incident damage so ordinary early faults are recoverable.
